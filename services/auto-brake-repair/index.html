In typical mixed driving conditions, pads last between 30,000 and 70,000 miles. Drivers who do a lot of city driving or frequent highway braking will go through pads faster. We measure pad thickness at every service visit and let you know where you stand. - question: Do I need to replace rotors when I replace brake pads? answer: Not always. If your rotors are within the manufacturer's minimum thickness specification and show no signs of warping, scoring, or heat damage, they can often be reused. We measure and inspect every rotor and give you an honest recommendation rather than automatically replacing parts that do not need it. - question: What causes brake pads to wear unevenly? answer: Uneven pad wear is usually caused by a sticking or seized caliper that keeps one pad pressed against the rotor even when you are not braking. It can also result from a worn slide pin, contaminated brake fluid, or a bent rotor. We diagnose the underlying cause rather than just replacing the worn parts. - question: How often should I have my brakes inspected? answer: We recommend a brake inspection at least once a year or every 12,000 miles, whichever comes first. We also inspect brakes visually at every oil change and flag anything that looks like it needs attention. - question: Is it safe to drive with a brake warning light on? answer: It depends on which light. A red brake warning light typically indicates low brake fluid or a hydraulic system issue and means you should stop driving and have the vehicle checked immediately. An amber ABS light means the anti-lock system has a fault but your base braking still works. Either way, get it diagnosed as soon as possible. - question: Do you service all vehicle makes and models? answer: Yes.
