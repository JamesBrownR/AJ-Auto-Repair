Signs Your Tires Need Service

Uneven Tread Wear

Wear on one edge indicates alignment problems. Center wear suggests over-inflation. Edge wear on both sides indicates under-inflation. Cupping or scalloping points to suspension issues.

Action: Get alignment and inspection immediately

Low Tread Depth

Tires below 4/32" tread depth lose traction in wet conditions. Florida's frequent rain makes adequate tread depth critical for safety. Legal minimum is 2/32" but unsafe before that.

Action: Replace tires at 4/32" for optimal safety

Vibration While Driving

Vibration at highway speeds indicates unbalanced tires or damaged suspension components. Continuing to drive causes additional damage and uneven tire wear.

Action: Get balancing and inspection promptly

Vehicle Pulls to One Side

Consistent pulling left or right indicates alignment problems, uneven tire pressure, or brake issues. This creates dangerous handling and accelerates tire wear.

Action: Schedule alignment check and tire inspection

Visible Damage

Bulges, cracks in sidewalls, exposed cords, or punctures larger than 1/4" require immediate attention. These conditions can cause sudden tire failure.

Action: Do not drive - have vehicle towed if necessary

Age

Tires degrade over time regardless of tread depth. Most manufacturers recommend replacement after 6 years. Check the DOT code on your tire sidewall for manufacture date.

Action: Replace tires over 6 years old

Tire Maintenance Tips for Florida Drivers

Check Pressure Monthly

Check tire pressure at least once per month when tires are cold. Florida's temperature fluctuations affect pressure significantly.

Rotate Every 5,000 Miles

Regular rotation prevents uneven wear and extends tire life. Combine with oil changes for convenient scheduling.

Inspect Regularly

Look for cracks, bulges, embedded objects, and uneven wear. Catch problems early before they become dangerous or expensive.

Avoid Potholes

Florida roads can have potholes that damage tires and alignment. Drive carefully and avoid impact when possible.

How to Check Tire Pressure

The Most Important Maintenance Task

Proper tire pressure affects safety, fuel economy, tire wear, and handling. Under-inflated tires are the most common tire problem and cause excessive heat buildup, rapid wear, and potential failure.

  1. Find Correct Pressure: Check the sticker inside your driver's door jamb, NOT the number on your tire (that's maximum pressure)
  2. Check When Cold: Check pressure when tires haven't been driven for at least 3 hours
  3. Use Quality Gauge: Digital gauges are most accurate; cheap stick gauges are unreliable
  4. Check All Four: Check all four tires plus your spare
  5. Adjust as Needed: Add or release air to match recommended pressure
  6. Check Monthly: Pressure drops 1 PSI per month naturally, more in temperature changes

Florida temperatures can cause 30-40°F swings between summer afternoons and winter mornings, significantly affecting tire pressure. Check more frequently during seasonal transitions.

Understanding Tread Depth

10/32"

New Tire

New tires typically have 10/32" to 12/32" of tread depth. Maximum traction and water evacuation.

EXCELLENT
6/32"

Good Condition

Tires still have good tread and plenty of life remaining. Continue regular rotation and maintenance.

GOOD
4/32"

Start Planning

Traction in wet conditions begins to decrease. In Florida's frequent rain, consider replacement soon.

CAUTION
2/32"

Replace Now

Legal minimum but unsafe. Poor wet traction, long stopping distances. Replace immediately.

REPLACE

The Penny Test: Insert a penny into tread with Lincoln's head upside down. If you can see the top of his head, tires are below 2/32" and must be replaced. For better safety, use a quarter - if you can see the top of Washington's head, tires are at or below 4/32" and should be replaced soon.

Seasonal Tire Care for Central Florida

Summer (May-September)

  • Check pressure more frequently - heat increases pressure
  • Ensure adequate tread for rain season
  • Inspect for heat-related damage and cracking
  • Avoid parking in direct sun for extended periods
  • Monitor for signs of overheating

Fall/Winter (October-April)

  • Check pressure after temperature drops
  • Cold weather reduces pressure 1 PSI per 10°F
  • Inspect tires before holiday travel
  • Ensure adequate tread for occasional wet conditions
  • Consider tire rotation before high-mileage season

Hurricane Season (June-November)

  • Maintain tires in good condition for potential evacuation
  • Check spare tire pressure and condition
  • Ensure adequate tread for heavy rain
  • Consider rotating before hurricane season begins
  • Keep tire repair kit or emergency sealant in vehicle
