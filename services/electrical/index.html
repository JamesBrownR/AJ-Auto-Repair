A failing battery may start the car fine when it is fully charged but lose charge quickly. A failing alternator cannot recharge the battery while the engine is running, so the car may start but will eventually die while driving. We test both together so you get the right answer the first time. - question: How long does a car battery last in Florida? answer: Florida's heat is hard on batteries. The average battery life here is three to four years, shorter than the national average of four to five years. Heat accelerates the internal chemical breakdown that causes batteries to fail. We recommend testing your battery every year once it is past the two-year mark. - question: Why do my fuses keep blowing? answer: A fuse blows when a circuit draws more current than it is rated for. If a fuse blows once and the replacement holds, that is usually just an anomaly. If the same fuse keeps blowing, there is an underlying short circuit or overloaded component that needs to be found and fixed. Repeatedly replacing the fuse without diagnosing the cause can lead to wiring damage or a fire. - question: Can you fix wiring damage from rodents? answer: Yes.
