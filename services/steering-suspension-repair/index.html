Your suspension keeps your tires in contact with the road and absorbs the impact of every bump and imperfection in the pavement. Your steering system translates your input into directional control. When either wears out or fails, handling, ride quality, and safety are all affected.

Push down firmly on each corner of the vehicle and let go. If it bounces more than once or twice before settling, the shocks or struts are likely worn. Other signs include excessive body roll in turns, a rough or floating ride, or uneven tire wear. We inspect them as part of any suspension service. - question: Does wheel alignment need to be done after suspension work? answer: Yes, almost always. Replacing tie rods, control arms, ball joints, or struts changes the geometry of the suspension. Aligning after these repairs is not optional if you want the tires to wear evenly and the vehicle to track straight. - question: How often should I get a wheel alignment? answer: Most manufacturers recommend checking alignment every 12,000 miles or once a year.
