A small leak at the back of the system near the tailpipe is less immediately dangerous than a leak closer to the engine or cabin. Exhaust gases contain carbon monoxide, which is odorless and can enter the cabin without you noticing. If you smell exhaust fumes inside the vehicle at any point, stop driving and have it inspected as soon as possible. - question: Why is my check engine light on after a catalytic converter code? answer: Catalytic converter codes, typically P0420 or P0430, mean the converter's efficiency has dropped below the threshold the vehicle's computer expects. This can be caused by a failing converter, an upstream exhaust leak that skews the oxygen sensor readings, or a failing oxygen sensor itself. We diagnose which is actually causing the code before recommending a replacement. - question: How long does a catalytic converter last? answer: Most catalytic converters last the life of the vehicle under normal conditions, often 100,000 miles or more. Premature failure is usually caused by engine misfires that send unburned fuel into the converter and overheat it, oil or coolant contamination from an engine leak, or physical damage from road debris. - question: Will an exhaust leak affect my gas mileage? answer: Yes. An exhaust leak upstream of the downstream oxygen sensor causes the engine management system to misread the exhaust composition and adjust fuel delivery incorrectly. This can reduce fuel economy noticeably. A clogged catalytic converter also creates back pressure that makes the engine work harder and burn more fuel. - question: My exhaust is rattling underneath the car.
