However, some counties have required it in the past and requirements can vary by vehicle type, age, and registration situation. If you have been told your vehicle needs an emissions test, we can perform it and address any issues found. - question: Why did my vehicle fail emissions with no check engine light? answer: OBD-II inspections check both active fault codes and readiness monitor status. If the monitors are not complete, the vehicle fails even without a check engine light. This commonly happens after a battery replacement or after codes were recently cleared. The vehicle needs to complete a drive cycle so the monitors can run and reach a ready state. - question: Can you clear the check engine light so my car passes? answer: Clearing the code without fixing the underlying problem will not result in a pass. The code clears the readiness monitors at the same time, which causes the vehicle to fail the OBD readiness check. The monitors need time to reset, and the original fault will return. We fix the actual problem so the light stays off and the monitors complete normally. - question: How long does it take to complete the readiness monitors after a repair? answer: It depends on the vehicle and which monitors need to complete. Some reset within a short drive. Others, such as the EVAP monitor, require specific driving conditions including cold starts, highway speeds, and stop-and-go driving. We run the appropriate drive cycle in-house when time permits, or advise you on the drive pattern needed to complete the monitors before your test. - question: My catalytic converter was recently stolen and replaced. A universal or undersized converter may not achieve the efficiency the vehicle's computer expects and could still trigger a fault code. We verify correct converter installation and test efficiency before you go in for inspection. - question: Do you service all makes and models? answer: Yes.
